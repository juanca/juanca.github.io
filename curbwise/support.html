Why didn't I get a notification?

  • Check Settings → Notifications → curbwise. Notifications need to be allowed; if you tapped "Don't Allow" on the first prompt, the warning notifications won't fire.
  • Curbwise schedules notifications 15 minutes before the next sweep. If you saved your spot less than 15 minutes before sweeping starts, the notification fires immediately.
  • If you tapped "I moved my car" on a previous notification, the next one is canceled until you save again.

The card names the wrong block

Curbwise matches against blockfaces within 150 metres of where your phone reports you. In urban canyons (downtown, between tall buildings) GPS can drift further than that, so the pin can land a block off.

You don't have to accept the match: the pin is fixed at the centre of the map, so pan the map until it sits on the curb you actually parked at and the card below updates to that block before you tap Save here.

The schedule on the sign is different from what curbwise shows

Curbwise reads from the cities' own open data — DataSF for San Francisco, the City of Oakland's map service for Oakland — which reflects the rule but isn't always the very latest update. The sign on the curb wins. If you spot a mismatch, email me with the block name and what the sign says — that helps build the freshness signal.

What about red curbs, meters, or RPP zones?

Curbwise covers street sweeping and posted hourly time limits in v1. It does not cover painted curbs (red, yellow, white, green, blue), metered parking hours, residential permit zone enforcement, garages, or temporary signage.

Always glance at the curb and read the sign — Curbwise is a heads-up, not a guarantee. If a tow truck shows up, the sign wins.

Does this work outside San Francisco and Oakland?

Those are the two cities curbwise covers today. Other Bay Area cities (Berkeley, Daly City, etc.) and beyond are on the roadmap, prioritized by where requests come from.

What about CarPlay?

CarPlay is live. Plug in and curbwise appears on the car's screen with the nearby blocks listed by how soon each one sweeps, a "Save here" button for the closest match, and a map view of the same candidates. If you'd rather not touch anything, curbwise can also save the spot on its own the moment you unplug.

What costs money?

Saving your spot, the 15-minute sweep warning, time-limit warnings, the map, CarPlay, Siri, the widget, and the Apple Watch app are all free, with no account to make.

Curbwise Plus — $1 per month or $10 per year — adds tracking more than one car, sharing a car with a partner, watching a block, custom (earlier) sweep reminders, and the real alarm. Tapping any of those opens the purchase screen; nothing you already rely on moves behind it.

I subscribed, but Plus isn't unlocked

Open the Curbwise Plus screen and tap Restore Purchases. That re-checks the Apple Account signed into the device — if you subscribed on a different Apple Account, sign into that one and try again. If it still says nothing to restore, email me with the date of the purchase.

How do I cancel?

Settings → your name at the top → Subscriptions → curbwise → Cancel Subscription. Cancelling stops the renewal; Plus keeps working until the period you already paid for runs out. Refunds are handled by Apple, at reportaproblem.apple.com, not by me.

The alarm didn't sound

The real alarm is a Curbwise Plus feature and it needs iOS 26 or later — on anything older, that last reminder arrives as an ordinary notification instead. Check that it's switched on in Manage → Sweep reminders, and that you granted curbwise permission to sound alarms when it asked. Earlier heads-ups in the same sequence are always ordinary notifications by design.
